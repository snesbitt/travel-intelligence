Roadmap · built in deliberate phases

Travel Intelligence is one build old.

Phase 1 is live: the full input card, the six-dimension scoring engine, and every output page, deployed as a static site. What's next is listed honestly below — including the parts that require your explicit go-ahead before they happen.

01 the optimization engine Live

A deterministic, client-side scoring model: every candidate itinerary is scored 0–100 on Cost, Comfort, Upgrade Potential, Award Value, Flexibility, and Risk, weighted by your ranked priority list. Outputs an Executive Summary, Best Recommendation, Top Five Alternatives comparison table, Upgrade Strategy, Award Strategy (with a genuinely-strong redemption bar, not a blanket "use your miles"), Purchase Timing, and 5–8 input-specific Travel Advisor Insights.

Every recommendation explains its own weighting instead of hiding behind a single score.

  • JUL 2026Full Input Card built: trip, flight preferences, ranked optimization priority, credit cards, 13 airline loyalty programs, and airport flexibility — matching the fields specified in the original agent brief.
  • JUL 2026Hotel loyalty programs removed from the Input Card for now — the scoring engine wasn't using them, and a checkbox that doesn't affect the result is worse than no checkbox. Revisit once hotel signal actually feeds the model.
  • JUL 2026Pricing model reworked after an early version clustered nearly every Business-class result at the same floor price regardless of carrier or route — replaced with per-cabin base prices, seeded variation, and cabin-appropriate floors so the spread looks like a real fare set rather than a repeated number.
  • JUL 2026House style adopted from the sibling portfolio: cream/serif shell, persistent nav, pill-nav, phase/step page patterns — matching thefitnesslog.org and streamingscout.org rather than a one-off look.
  • JUL 2026Roame/TPG iteration shipped: miles-or-cash pricing on every candidate (either/or, flips at the 1.6¢/mile bar), a Points Intelligence panel with TPG's dated July 2026 valuations and active transfer bonuses, seven concrete upgrade tactics (bid 20–40% of the fare gap, bid-window timing, check-in counter-offers, miles+copay, two creative plays), the Optimization Priority card simplified to dropdowns, a sort-by control on the alternatives table, and a saved wallet — cards and balances persist in the browser, editable and re-savable as figures change.

05 live fare & award data Live (fares)

Live fares are running: a Netlify Function (/api/fares) searches real Google Flights results via SerpApi on every run, and the frontend scores them through the same six-dimension engine. The credential was created and installed by Susan directly — credentials and spend are never taken on the agent's behalf. If the backend is ever unreachable, the site falls back to the documented model and says so in the banner rather than failing silently. Award-space search (seats.aero-style availability) is the remaining data gap.

The recommendation stops being a documented estimate and becomes a real, bookable answer.

  • JUL 2026Fare backend shipped: provider-proxy Function with honest 503 fallback when unconfigured, city→IATA mapping, and live offers scored by the identical weighting engine — real prices, same legible framework. Route geometry also fixed model mode: flight times and fare scaling now derive from real great-circle distances (a nonstop to Rome reads ~12h, not a fabricated short-haul number).
  • JUL 2026Provider pivot, one day later: Amadeus decommissioned its self-service portal on Jul 17 — discovered when attempting the signup, verified on their site. Backend v2 swapped to Google Flights data via SerpApi (self-serve key, free tier), same honest-fallback contract. Netlify was also linked to the GitHub repo, closing phase 03: pushes now auto-deploy. Live fares verified end-to-end the same week.
  • JUL 2026Economy → Business playbook: the Upgrade Strategy now opens with three priced routes to Business for the specific trip (Premium Economy ladder, direct bid, miles + copay checked against saved balances) and a dated timeline targeting Business — replacing one-cabin-up advice that buried the most common real goal. Trip purpose simplified to Vacation / Business / Both; via/stopover routing, a travelers stepper, and a $2,000 default budget shipped alongside.
  • JUL 2026Density pass: homepage header compressed to a one-line status banner (details moved to About/Guide), and long cards — loyalty balances, TPG valuations, the upgrade timeline — now collapse to their high-signal rows with explicit "Show all N" toggles.

07 saved preferences & trip memory Live

Remembering preferred airlines, seats, hotels, upgrade successes, disliked airports, connection tolerance, and typical travel style across sessions, so later recommendations get more personalized instead of starting from the same defaults every time — matching the North Star in the original agent brief.

The agent gets better at optimizing for you specifically, trip over trip.

  • JUL 2026First slice shipped early by direct request: the saved wallet (credit cards + 13 loyalty balances) now persists in the browser via localStorage and reloads automatically — the first piece of cross-session memory, ahead of the fuller preference profile this phase describes.
  • JUL 2026The fare watcher shipped — the audit's "one big bet." Watch a trip (max 3) and a scheduled backend re-searches it daily, with day-over-day fare-floor deltas on the homepage. The cap exists because each watched trip spends one metered search per day; raising it, or adding email/push alerts (which need a new credential), stays an explicit approval decision. Shipped alongside: the upgrade timeline exports to calendar (.ics), the winning itinerary links straight to the carrier's booking site, and a service worker gives the installed app an offline shell.
  • JUL 2026Watched-trip cap lowered from 3 to 2, for quota safety: a fixed doc error found the real SerpApi free tier is 100 searches/month, not 250 — at the old cap of 3, the daily watcher alone spent ~90 of those, leaving only ~10/month for manual "Run the optimization" calls. At 2, the watcher spends ~60/month, leaving a comfortable ~40/month of headroom. Fewer trips can be watched at once as a direct result; nothing else about the watcher changed.
  • JUL 2026The fuller preference profile shipped: cabin, max stops, preferred/avoided airlines, preferred alliances, departure/arrival time preferences, airports to avoid, preferred seat, preferred hotel brand(s), and typical travel style all now auto-save and reload, same as the wallet. A saved travel style (Relaxed/Efficient/Luxury) only steers the suggested optimization lens when nothing about the specific trip already picked one — a saved default never overrides real trip signals like a tight budget. A saved "airports to avoid" list is checked against the trip's own fields (origin, destination, preferred/nearby airports, via city) — honestly, it can't check a specific connection airport, since the model only tracks a stop count, not named connection airports. A new "upgrade wins" log lets you record past successful upgrades; if one mentions the recommended carrier, the insights say so. Preferred seat and hotel brand are saved for reference only — this site doesn't score seats or hotels.

Phases are sequential. A later phase isn't started early, and nothing gets added to an earlier phase just because it would be easy to slot in.
