01 the optimization engine Live

A deterministic, client-side scoring model: every candidate itinerary is scored 0–100 on Cost, Comfort, Upgrade Potential, Award Value, Flexibility, Risk, and Time, weighted by your ranked priority list. Outputs an Executive Summary, Best Recommendation, Top Five Alternatives comparison table, Upgrade Strategy, Award Strategy (with a genuinely-strong redemption bar, not a blanket "use your miles"), Purchase Timing, and 5–8 input-specific Travel Advisor Insights.

Every recommendation explains its own weighting instead of hiding behind a single score.

  • JUL 2026Built the full Input Card: trip details, flight preferences, ranked optimization priority, credit cards, 13 airline loyalty programs, and airport flexibility. This matches the fields specified in the original agent brief.
  • JUL 2026Removed hotel loyalty programs from the Input Card for now. The scoring engine was not using them, and a checkbox that does not affect the result is worse than no checkbox. They return once hotel signal actually feeds the model.
  • JUL 2026Reworked the pricing model. An early version priced nearly every Business-class result at the same floor, regardless of carrier or route. The new model uses per-cabin base prices with seeded variation and cabin-appropriate floors, so the spread reads like a real fare set instead of a repeated number.
  • JUL 2026Adopted the shared portfolio design: cream and serif styling, persistent navigation, and common page patterns. The site now matches thefitnesslog.org and streamingscout.org instead of looking like a one-off.
  • JUL 2026Shipped a major round of pricing and points features. Every candidate now shows a miles-or-cash price, flipping to miles only when the value clears the 1.6¢-per-mile bar. A new Points Intelligence panel carries TPG's dated July 2026 valuations and active transfer bonuses, and the upgrade guidance lists seven concrete tactics, from bidding 20–40% of the fare gap to miles-plus-copay. The priority card was simplified to dropdowns, the alternatives table gained a sort control, and a saved wallet keeps cards and balances in the browser, editable as figures change.

05 live fare & award data Live (fares + scans)

Live fares are running: a small server function searches real Google Flights results on every run, merges in any fares you've scanned yourself from Kayak or Booking, and scores them through the same seven-dimension engine. The credential was created and installed by Susan directly; credentials and spend are never taken on the agent's behalf. If nothing real comes back for a route, the site says so plainly and shows no numbers, rather than guessing. Award-space search (seats.aero-style availability) is the remaining data gap.

The recommendation stops being a documented estimate and becomes a real, bookable answer.

  • JUL 2026Shipped the fare backend. Live offers are scored by the identical weighting engine: real prices, same legible framework. If the backend is unconfigured, the site returns an honest 503 fallback rather than failing silently. Flight times and fare scaling in model mode were also corrected to derive from real great-circle distances, so a nonstop to Rome reads about 12 hours, not a fabricated short-haul number.
  • JUL 2026Switched fare providers one day later. Amadeus decommissioned its self-service portal on Jul 17, discovered when attempting the signup and verified on their site. Backend v2 now uses Google Flights data via SerpApi (a self-serve key on the free tier), with the same honest-fallback contract. Netlify was also linked to the GitHub repo, closing phase 03, and live fares were verified end to end the same week.
  • JUL 2026Rebuilt the Upgrade Strategy around the most common real goal: getting from Economy to Business. It now opens with three priced routes to Business for the specific trip (a Premium Economy ladder, a direct bid, and miles plus copay checked against saved balances) plus a dated timeline. The old one-cabin-up advice buried this goal. Trip purpose was simplified to Vacation, Business, or Both, and stopover routing, a travelers stepper, and a $2,000 default budget shipped alongside.
  • JUL 2026Tightened the homepage. The header is now a one-line status banner, with details moved to About and Guide. Long cards (loyalty balances, TPG valuations, the upgrade timeline) collapse to their high-signal rows, with explicit "Show all N" toggles.
  • JUL 2026Added a second live price source. Fares scanned from your own logged-in Kayak and Booking searches now merge into the same shortlist with a labeled source pill, alongside Google's price history and a buy, wait, or monitor verdict. Every alternative, not just the top pick, now links out to review and buy: Google Flights on every row, a verified aa.com link on American rows, and a verified Kayak link on scanned rows. Booking.com's own link format was tested live in three shapes and none prefill the route, so those rows fall back to Google Flights rather than ship a link that does not work.
  • JUL 2026Expanded global city coverage and fixed a real bug. The whole Baltic, Balkan, Caucasus, and Central Asia region (Estonia included) was silently unmapped: the same bug class as the Greenville incident. The fix added capital-city codes and locked together two lookup tables that could previously disagree, which had let a bare country name resolve to a bookable airport code while its distance estimate silently came back blank.
  • JUL 2026Made failed searches end usefully. A depart date beyond the roughly 330-day airline booking horizon is not a failure; the empty state now says so by name, including how many days out it is and the date fares should open, and skips a wasted retry. Any other failed search gets one automatic retry, then an actionable empty state: a Retry button plus verified Google Flights and Kayak links, never a dead end.

07 saved preferences & trip memory Live

Remembering preferred airlines, seats, hotels, upgrade successes, disliked airports, connection tolerance, and typical travel style across sessions, so later recommendations get more personalized instead of starting from the same defaults every time. This matches the North Star in the original agent brief.

The agent gets better at optimizing for you specifically, trip over trip.

  • JUL 2026Shipped the first slice early, by direct request. The saved wallet (credit cards plus 13 loyalty balances) now persists in the browser via localStorage and reloads automatically. It is the first piece of cross-session memory, ahead of the fuller preference profile this phase describes.
  • JUL 2026Shipped the fare watcher, the audit's "one big bet." Watch a trip (max 3) and a scheduled backend re-searches it daily, showing day-over-day fare-floor changes on the homepage. The cap exists because each watched trip spends one metered search per day; raising it, or adding email or push alerts (which need a new credential), stays an explicit approval decision. Also shipped: the upgrade timeline exports to calendar (.ics), the winning itinerary links straight to the carrier's booking site, and a service worker gives the installed app an offline shell.
  • JUL 2026Lowered the watched-trip cap from 3 to 2 for quota safety. A corrected documentation error revealed the real SerpApi free tier is 100 searches per month, not 250. At the old cap of 3, the daily watcher alone spent about 90 of those, leaving only about 10 per month for manual "Run the optimization" calls. At 2, the watcher spends about 60, leaving a comfortable 40 of headroom. Fewer trips can be watched at once as a direct result; nothing else about the watcher changed.
  • JUL 2026Raised the cap back to 3 at Susan's direct request. The quota math is restated plainly at the top of watch.mjs: about 90 searches per month for the watcher, leaving about 10 for manual runs. That is tight, and it is the trade Susan chose knowingly. If manual runs start hitting the monthly limit, dropping back to 2 (or stretching the check cadence) is a one-line reversal.
  • JUL 2026Shipped the fuller preference profile. Cabin, max stops, preferred and avoided airlines, preferred alliances, departure and arrival time preferences, airports to avoid, preferred seat, preferred hotel brands, and typical travel style all now auto-save and reload, same as the wallet. A saved travel style (Relaxed, Efficient, or Luxury) only steers the suggested optimization lens when nothing about the specific trip already picked one; a saved default never overrides real trip signals like a tight budget. One honest limit: the airports-to-avoid list is checked against the trip's own fields but cannot check a specific connection airport, since the model only tracks a stop count, not named connection airports. A new "upgrade wins" log records past successful upgrades; if one mentions the recommended carrier, the insights say so. Preferred seat and hotel brand are saved for reference only, since this site does not score seats or hotels.
08 protect watched trips Roadmap

Watching a trip (max 3) spends a metered daily search and persists to the shared store the scheduled watcher reads from. That store is currently open to anyone with the link, with no auth: the same model Streaming Scout's dismiss endpoint uses. It is worth gating behind a shared edit key, remembered per device after one entry (the same pattern across the portfolio), so a shared link cannot quietly add or drop a watched trip and spend the limited daily quota. Everything else, running the optimizer and browsing results, stays open.

A shared link can't quietly spend your watched-trip slots or swap what's being tracked.

09 2026 simplicity pass Live

A deliberate simplification pass: the input form was cut to four essential fields (from, to, dates, what matters most), with everything else folded into an optional "Refine" panel that remembers whether you left it open. That panel holds the via city, preferred and nearby airports, cabin, budget, stops, airlines, and wallet. The old synthetic/modeled-fare fallback was deleted outright: when no live fare or scan data exists for a route, the site now says so plainly instead of ever showing a number nobody actually quoted. Fraunces and IBM Plex were also moved off Google Fonts onto self-hosted files with long-lived caching, across all five sites in the portfolio.

Fewer fields to fill in, and every number on screen is a real quote or scan, never a guess dressed up as one.

  • JUL 2026Cut the trip form to four always-visible fields, moving via city, airports, cabin, budget, stops, preferred and avoided airlines, and the wallet into a collapsible "Refine" panel that remembers its open or closed state. After feedback that two load-bearing controls had been folded away too aggressively, the date ± flexibility pills and the secondary "then weigh" priority were restored to the always-visible Trip card.
  • JUL 2026Deleted the synthetic candidate generator (seeded random fares, invented aircraft and alliances). Live search and Susan's own scanned fares are now the only data path; when neither has coverage for a route, the results panel says so honestly instead of falling back to a modeled guess.
  • JUL 2026Self-hosted Fraunces and IBM Plex Sans/Mono as local font files with immutable caching, removing the Google Fonts request on every page load.
  • JUL 2026Tightened vertical spacing on the results panel, the run-optimization area, and the watched-trips card, with a two-line price layout, so more fits above the fold without a scroll.

Phases are sequential. A later phase isn't started early, and nothing gets added to an earlier phase just because it would be easy to slot in.
