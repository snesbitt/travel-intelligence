Roadmap · built in deliberate phases

Nine phases live. Four to go.

The optimization engine, live fare and award data, the real domain, saved preferences and trip memory, and the Concert Radar cross-check with Vinyl Scout are all live today. What's next is listed honestly below, including the parts that require your explicit go-ahead before they happen.

1

The optimization engine

Live

Every recommendation explains its own weighting

A deterministic scoring model: every itinerary is scored 0–100 on Cost, Comfort, Upgrade Potential, Award Value, Flexibility, Risk, and Time, weighted by your ranked priorities. Outputs a recommendation, a Top Five comparison, upgrade and award strategy, purchase timing, and trip-specific insights. Live fares now flow through the same engine (see phase 5), and a Points Intelligence panel carries dated valuations and transfer bonuses.

2

The four-page site

Live

Something to run a real trip through

Index (the optimizer itself), About, Guide, and this Roadmap: a static, no-build site deployed to Netlify, styled to match the rest of the portfolio.

3

GitHub repo

Live

Parity with the rest of the portfolio's deploy pipeline

The same continuous-deployment model as the sibling sites: push to main, Netlify auto-deploys. Commits are made from Cowork sessions and pushed from Susan's own machine, since no GitHub credential lives in the cloud session.

4

iOS app

Live

One tap from the Home Screen before a trip

An installable version that adds itself to the Home Screen with its own icon and no browser chrome. Same site, no separate codebase: a web app manifest and an icon set, nothing more.

5

Live fare & award data

Live (fares + scans)

A real, bookable answer, not a documented estimate

A server function searches real Google Flights results on every run, merges in fares you've scanned yourself from Kayak or Booking, and scores them through the same seven-dimension engine. Every alternative links out to review and buy. If nothing real comes back for a route, the site says so plainly rather than guessing. Award-space availability (seats.aero-style) is the remaining data gap.

6

A real domain

Live

One address, not a netlify.app subdomain

travelintelligence.org, registered and connected via Netlify DNS, matching the rest of the portfolio. TLS provisions automatically within a few minutes of connecting.

7

Saved preferences & trip memory

Live

Gets better at optimizing for you specifically, trip over trip

Avoided airlines, disliked airports, stops tolerance, and travel style now auto-save and reload across sessions, alongside the wallet (cards and 13 loyalty balances) — and actually reshape which offer ranks highest, not just what gets remembered. A saved travel style only steers the optimization lens when the trip itself hasn't already picked one. Preferred airlines, alliances, and departure/arrival time windows now feed real Comfort scoring too (2026-08-05), each visibly announced in the results insights whenever it's active. Preferred seat, preferred hotel brand, checked bags, and carry-on-only remain reference only: this site doesn't score seats, hotels, or baggage — live fare data simply doesn't carry that information. The fare watcher (up to 3 trips) re-searches daily and shows day-over-day price changes, budgeted against SerpApi's real 100-search monthly quota.

9

2026 simplicity pass

Live

Every number on screen is a real quote, never a guess

The input form was cut to four essential fields, with everything else folded into an optional Refine panel that remembers whether you left it open. The synthetic modeled-fare fallback was deleted outright: with no live fare or scan data for a route, the site now says so instead of showing an invented number. Fonts moved to self-hosted files across the whole portfolio.

5b

Region-to-region search

Future

Ask "how much to get to Europe" without picking a city first

Allow a destination as a region name ("Europe," "East Asia," "Caribbean"). The engine would score 2–3 hub options for the region instead of forcing a single city.

5c

Date-range score sweep

Future

See the cheapest date in one chart, not ten re-runs

Sample the top itinerary's score and price across a 30–60 day window at 3–5 day intervals, and chart it, instead of re-running the optimizer by hand to find the cheapest date.

7a

Loyalty balance integration

Future

See instantly whether your miles cover the trip

Check the saved loyalty-program balance against the redemption this itinerary needs, and say plainly whether it's enough, or how many more you'd need.

8

Protect watched trips

Future

A shared link can't quietly spend your watch slots

Watching a trip spends a metered daily search against a shared store that's currently open to anyone with the link. Worth gating behind a shared edit key, remembered per device, the same pattern used across the portfolio. Running the optimizer and browsing results stay open.

10

Concert Radar hooks

Live

Know who's playing where you're headed, before you go

A bidirectional link with Vinyl Scout's Concert Radar (vinylscout.org): watch a trip here and see whether an artist you're interested in — catalog, wishlist, and Concert Radar's own venue-scraped listings, not just the curated Watching list — is playing near your destination during your dates, and the same match shows up on Concert Radar's own page too ("playing where you're headed"). This site's half is a new GET /api/watched-trips read endpoint returning destination city, coordinates, and date range only — deliberately no fares or points data. Vinyl Scout's half checks its catalog/wishlist/watching artists against a location+date-window SeatGeek query plus its own venue scrape, within a fixed ~25-mile radius. Built 2026-08-05, the day after the technical plan — each Watched Trips row now shows a real match inline when one exists, best-effort so a slow or unreachable Vinyl Scout never blocks this page's own data. Full build detail in this repo's own CLAUDE.md (2026-08-05 entry) and Vinyl Scout's PROJECT.md (Phase 10).
